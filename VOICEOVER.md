# Voiceover script — `walkthrough.mp4`

**Total: 2:08.** Timings match the cuts in the video exactly. Read at a normal
pace with real pauses; every line has a couple of seconds of headroom.

Tone: a duty officer explaining a tool to another duty officer. Dry, not
jokey. The humour is in the observations, not in the delivery — say the funny
lines completely straight and they land.

---

**0:00 – 0:11 · Wellington right now**

> This is Wellington, right now.
> Everything on one map — what people have reported, what they have
> photographed, what they are asking for, and what the Council has confirmed
> itself.
> One page. Not eleven tabs and a phone call.

**0:11 – 0:20 · Near me**

> You can narrow it to your own street.
> Type an address and it filters to a radius you choose.
> Your location never leaves your browser — we shipped the whole Wellington
> street index with the page, so nobody has to tell a server where they live
> to find out if their road is flooded.

**0:20 – 0:31 · Evidence stacks**

> Five people photograph the same flooded road.
> Most systems would call that four duplicates and tidy them away.
> We call it five witnesses. The count *is* the signal — so it stacks, and
> the circle gets bigger.

**0:31 – 0:44 · Two questions**

> When you ask for help, the Council answers two separate questions.
> Are we coming — and roughly when.
> Separate on purpose. "Likely, but not tonight" is useful. "In progress" is
> not.
> And "we can't get to this" is a real answer, with a reason attached. During
> a real event it is sometimes the true one, and saying it early lets a
> neighbour step in instead of everyone waiting.

**0:44 – 0:54 · Reporting**

> Reporting takes one line.
> No account, no password, no download. In an emergency, a login screen is a
> barrier between you and the information the Council needs.
> If your photo still knows where it was taken, it places its own pin — and
> we strip that metadata off the copy we publish, so reporting a flood does
> not publish where you live.

**0:54 – 1:06 · The reference code**

> You get a reference code. That is the whole login.
> The status updates itself — received, being checked, on the way, crew on
> site, fixed.
> A third of council call-centre volume is people asking what happened to the
> thing they reported. Every one of those calls is someone who already helped
> and got nothing back.
> And you can answer back. "It's cleared" releases a crew — nothing else in
> the system could ever tell them that.

**1:06 – 1:15 · Updates**

> Updates from the agencies working the event, tagged by who said it.
> Ten categories. Evacuation is first, deliberately — it is the highest-stakes
> thing a council ever says, and it should never look like one option among
> ten.

**1:15 – 1:25 · One page for comms**

> Neighbourhood boards, inter-agency channels, and Council updates —
> merged into one page.
> Because somebody standing in the rain wants one place that answers "what is
> being said", not four tabs checked in turn.

**1:25 – 1:35 · Access cards**

> Officials sign in with a code printed on a card in their wallet.
> No email, no text message, no single sign-on.
> During an emergency, those are precisely the things that fail. A card in a
> wallet does not need the internet to still be a card.

**1:35 – 1:46 · Five stages**

> Five stages, each an observable fact with a time on it.
> "On the way" and "crew on site" sound similar right up until you are the one
> waiting, standing in it.
> A card can only ever issue a card below its own level — so delegation always
> loses privilege, and a lost card can never manufacture its own replacement.

**1:46 – 1:55 · Comms hub**

> Eight agencies on one screen.
> Anyone can read it. Posting needs a card — so nobody can put words in Fire
> and Emergency's mouth, which felt worth preventing.

**1:55 – 2:08 · Climate signal**

> And a climate signal, stated honestly.
> Today's reports correlated against the flood hazard areas the Council has
> already mapped, and the deprivation of the areas carrying the impact.
> Correlation across one event. Not a trend — with six hours of data, a trend
> would be fiction, and this is a product about not doing that.
>
> It runs on the standard library. No framework, no map server, no build step.
> Clone it and it works offline.

## Recording notes

- **Pauses matter more than pace.** The lines are short on purpose; let each
  land before the next.
- **Do not lean on the jokes.** "Which felt worth preventing" and "standing in
  it" only work delivered flat.
- The video is silent, so you can record straight over it in any editor, or
  read live to it.
- If you would rather not record: the captions carry the whole story on their
  own. The video is complete without narration.
