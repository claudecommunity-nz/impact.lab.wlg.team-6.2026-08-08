The problem

Information flows out.
It doesn't flow back.

  • Reports arrive through several unrelated channels and may never reach the people who can use them
  • A third of call-centre volume is "what happened to the thing I reported?"
  • Every one of those calls is somebody who already helped, and got nothing back

The graded wording of Problem 02 is that communities see that their information has been received. That's the part everyone skips.

The loop · Report → My reports

One line in.
An answer back.

  • Report takes one sentence — no account, no password, no download
  • You get a reference code. That is the whole login
  • It acknowledges itself — no human has to be free for that
  • And you can answer back: still happening, getting worse, it's cleared

"It's cleared" releases a crew. Nothing else in the system could ever tell WCC that.

The loop · WCC queue

Five stages you can
actually observe.

Received → Being checked →
On the way → Crew on site → Fixed

"Responding" was doing too much work. A crew driving across town and a crew already on site are wildly different if you're the one waiting. Each stage is a fact with a time on it, not an estimate.

Live map · Evidence

Evidence stacks.
It doesn't dedupe.

5 photos of one flooded road
= 5 witnesses

  • Most systems collapse duplicates to reduce clutter — optimising for a reader
  • A duty officer is deciding whether it's real. Repetition is the evidence
Ask for help

Two questions,
answered separately.

  • Are we coming?
    Confirmed · Likely · Unlikely · We can't
  • Roughly when?
    Within the hour · Today · 24 hours · No ETA

"Likely, but not before tomorrow" is useful.

"In progress" is not.

"We can't get to this" is a first-class answer with a reason. During a real event it's sometimes the true one — and saying it early lets a neighbour step in instead of everyone waiting.

Photos · Community map

The photo already knows
where it was taken.

  • EXIF GPS places the pin itself — no map interaction in the rain
  • The same data is a precise record of where a person was
  • So: read it, show them what we read, then strip all metadata from the published copy

Reporting a flood must not publish where you live. Written in pure standard library — no Pillow, no exifread.

Access cards

A printed card
in a wallet.

  • No email. No SMS. No single sign-on. Those are the things that fail
  • Unambiguous alphabet + check character — a typo is rejected as a typo
  • A card can only ever issue a card below its own level

Delegation always loses privilege, so a leaked card can never manufacture its own replacement.

Moderation & trust

Nothing is ever deleted.

  • A flagged message leaves the public feed and a visible marker stays
  • The board notices who's useful — reports WCC acted on, not volume
  • A bot can grant moderator and nothing more — a role that can't mint cards

On a public emergency board, moderation that leaves a trace is the difference between trustworthy and censored.

Sustainability & climate

Correlation, stated
as correlation.

  • Today's reports against flood-hazard areas WCC already mapped
  • Median deprivation decile of the areas carrying the impact
  • Never called a trend — with six hours of data, that would be fiction

One map request instead of 300 tile fetches. No framework, no CDN, no build step — the thing that survives a dead network is also the thing that costs least to run.
