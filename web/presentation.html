Kitea

Wellington, seen.
A two-way information channel between communities and their council.
Impact Lab Wellington 2026 · Team 6 · Problem 02

The problem

A third of council call-centre volume during an event is status checks: “did you get my report? what's happening with it?” Nobody wants to make that call, or answer it. Every one is a failure of acknowledgment, not of information.

The idea

Model it on delivery tracking, not a support ticket. A resident reports in under a minute and watches it happen: received → reviewing → responding → resolved, pushed live. The council acknowledges by acting — one tap.

One live map

Community reports beside official agency data. Lenses: Everything, Official, Community, Mine.

Reported, tracked, verified

A council-verified report becomes official on the public map — provenance you can see.

The council console

Triage, hazard context, one-tap verify and status. Comms and Access tabs; emergency mode.

Any phone, one minute

No app, no login. A reference code is the only credential. Built for a stressed phone in bad weather.

Real data, honestly labelled

Live agency feeds in one operating picture: GWRC river & rain gauges, Waka Kotahi roads, GeoNet quakes, NEMA outages, MetService warnings, WREMO hubs. Every source shows its freshness; inference is never shown as fact.

Inclusion by construction

No account, no app store, no email. Fira Sans (the NZ Government type standard) served locally, so it works when the venue wifi doesn't. Report on someone's behalf. The deprivation signal shows the duty officer where need concentrates.

Built to be adopted

Zero runtime dependencies — stdlib + SQLite, runs on hardware a council already owns, inside its own boundary. Data never leaves. Append-only audit trail. Certified Production Ready against the Release Readiness Index.

Working as one

The horizon: agencies posting under their own names, the council requesting help that community and agencies answer, notification tiers, a standing skills register. In an emergency, everyone on one picture. In normal times, the same loop for everyday reports.
