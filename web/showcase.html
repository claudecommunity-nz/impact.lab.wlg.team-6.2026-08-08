Reports, photos, requests for help, offers, live feeds — and what the Council has confirmed itself.

  • Filter to your own street — your location never leaves your browser

One page. Not eleven tabs and a phone call.

The loop

One line in.
An answer back.

No account, no password. You get a reference code — that is the whole login.

  • Received → Being checked → On the way → Crew on site → Fixed
  • Each one an observable fact with a time on it, not an estimate
  • And the reporter can answer back — "it's cleared" releases a crew

A third of call-centre volume is people asking what happened to the thing they reported.

Evidence

Five witnesses,
not four duplicates.

Most systems collapse repeats to reduce clutter. That optimises for a reader.

  • A duty officer is deciding whether it is real — so repetition is the evidence
  • Photos place their own pin from EXIF, and we strip the metadata from the published copy
  • Reporting a flood must not publish where you live

The circle grows with corroboration. The count is the signal.

The hard part

Two questions,
answered honestly.

  • Are we coming? Confirmed · Likely · Unlikely · We can't
  • Roughly when? Within the hour · Today · 24 hours · No ETA
  • "We can't get to this" is a real answer, with a reason

During a real event it is sometimes the true one — and saying it early lets a neighbour step in, instead of everybody waiting on a status that never changes.
